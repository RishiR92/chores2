# Asmi Wine in France Film — V4 Story-First Rebuild

## Creative diagnosis
The current film feels clumsy because it behaves like a sequence of feature screens:
- the task appears before the viewer feels why it matters;
- large labels explain actions that the animation should make obvious;
- choices, call, email, receipt, and languages feel like separate slides;
- the product UI is framed as a generic card rather than one evolving Asmi task;
- empty backgrounds and oversized text make the film feel like a presentation instead of a lived story.

V4 will not patch those scenes. It will rebuild the film around one human moment: **you remembered four colleagues in Paris, wanted to send them genuinely good wine, and handed the whole problem to Asmi from New York.**

## The story — approximately 29 seconds

### 1. The reason — 0:00–0:03
Open on a real-feeling evening moment in New York, not a title card.

Four colleague names/photos briefly collect around a small calendar note: **“Paris team — tomorrow.”** A wine-bottle sketch or photo fragment establishes the gift instantly. The user opens the real iMessage thread with Asmi and sends:

> “send great wine to the four paris team tomorrow. local shop, not a generic gift box.”

No explanatory headline. The audience immediately understands who, what, where, and why.

### 2. Asmi accepts the problem — 0:03–0:05
Asmi replies in its real voice:

> “on it. finding a proper paris caviste.”

The blue message physically stretches into the Asmi task canvas. The camera follows the transformation so we never feel a scene reset.

### 3. Real research flow — 0:05–0:09
Inside one authentic Asmi canvas:
- task title: **wine for the paris team**;
- status changes from **researching** to **3 good options**;
- three local merchant options arrive one after another;
- each carries only useful choice cues: neighborhood, independent/local, delivery availability, and “phone order.”

Use restrained photographic crops of storefronts/bottles as visual context if they improve the shot. They sit inside the generated task view and never become a fake marketplace. If the image treatment weakens realism, use the actual text-first Asmi option rows instead.

Asmi marks one recommendation:

> “best small-producer selection. they take orders by phone.”

The user taps it. This is the only action after the original message.

### 4. The task moves to Paris — 0:09–0:11
The selected option becomes a moving parcel of information: **4 bottles / 4 people / tomorrow / Paris**. It travels across a fast, shallow New York-to-Paris route while the request’s essential words switch once from English to French.

This is a transition with purpose, not a standalone globe scene. It lasts under two seconds and lands directly inside the same Asmi task as a French call brief.

### 5. Asmi calls in French — 0:11–0:16
Show the real Asmi call progression inside the task canvas:

**queued → dialing → ringing → on call**

No fake Phone app, no parked iPhone mockup, and no visible live transcript. The canvas shows merchant identity, elapsed time, call state, and waveform only.

Use only the strongest five seconds of dialogue:
1. Realistic merchant pickup: **“Caves Bernard, bonjour.”**
2. Asmi’s compressed French request for four good bottles delivered tomorrow.
3. Merchant confirmation: **“Oui, bien sûr. Livraison demain après-midi.”**

The merchant sounds older, slower, and inside a working shop; Asmi is quicker and cleaner. One concise editorial English subtitle appears per speaker for sound-off clarity.

### 6. The call becomes follow-through — 0:16–0:20
The final waveform straightens into an email line inside the same task canvas. The activity timeline updates naturally:

- **call complete**
- **delivery confirmed for tomorrow afternoon**
- **French confirmation sent with four delivery addresses**

Open a compact Asmi email artifact—not a fake Mail app—showing the real useful fields and a short French body. The address rows populate rapidly, then the artifact folds back into the task history.

Copy stays inside the task. No separate “Asmi emails” headline.

### 7. The human payoff — 0:20–0:24
Return to iMessage, completing the same visual loop that began the story.

Asmi sends:

> “sorted. four local bottles. four paris addresses. delivery tomorrow afternoon.”

Then, in Asmi’s personality:

> “zero midnight french for you.”

Four compact delivery confirmations land beside the four original colleague identities. The opening problem is visibly resolved; this is the emotional payoff before the feature claim.

### 8. Earn the language feature — 0:24–0:27
The four confirmation slips rotate through a fast set of major languages while their layout remains stable. Call, message, and email marks travel through the same sentence and lock into three clear verbs:

**talks. texts. emails.**

Then the count lands hard:

**50+ languages.**

Supporting line:

**ask in yours. asmi handles theirs.**

The animation grows out of the completed French task, so it feels proven rather than advertised.

### 9. End — 0:27–0:29
The four delivery confirmations converge into the real Asmi logo.

Final copy:

**asmi**  
**your agent for the real world.**

Real iMessage and WhatsApp marks settle underneath with **“text asmi.”** End on one clean musical resolve.

## Visual direction

### Concept: A gift crosses the Atlantic
This should feel like a premium consumer launch film with the emotional clarity of a short story and the momentum of a dispatch sequence.

### Palette
Use a cinematic, bright-dark contrast rather than one flat background:
- luminous paper white for the user/iMessage world;
- deep bottle green for Paris and the merchant call;
- electric cobalt for Asmi actions;
- wine red only as a precise gift/selection accent;
- champagne yellow for confirmations and human warmth;
- near-black for type and depth.

The palette changes with the story but remains connected by cobalt action lines. Avoid full-screen burgundy banners, empty cream canvases, purple gradients, and generic tech glow.

### Typography
- **Display:** Bricolage Grotesque ExtraBold, only for the final language and brand statements.
- **Product surfaces:** authentic system UI typography matching Asmi and iMessage.
- **Utility data:** Noto Sans Mono for times, call states, addresses, and task events.
- No oversized sentence cards. Story copy appears in messages, recommendations, task events, and confirmations.

### Image treatment
- Use a small, cohesive set of realistic Paris wine-shop/storefront and bottle details when they help the viewer feel the real-world task.
- Keep images as cropped evidence inside the task flow or as brief depth layers during transitions—not decorative stock-photo backgrounds.
- Do not pretend that a photographed merchant or fulfilled order is documentary proof; the example remains illustrative.

## Motion language

### One continuous object
The original iMessage bubble is the film’s master object. It becomes:

```text
message → task canvas → merchant choice → call brief → waveform → email underline → completion message → language grid → logo
```

This removes the slide-deck feeling because every new state is caused by the previous one.

### Camera and transitions
- Camera glides forward through the message into Asmi’s canvas.
- Merchant options arrive as layered strips with different depth and velocity, not three equal cards fading in.
- Selecting the merchant pulls that row to the foreground; the other options shear away behind it.
- The selection line becomes the transatlantic route.
- The route pulse becomes the call stepper.
- The last waveform becomes the email underline.
- The sent email folds into the timeline and pushes the completed message back into iMessage.
- The four delivery confirmations fan outward, cycle languages, then collapse into the logo.

Use controlled inertial movement, crisp cuts on beats, shallow parallax, short directional trails, and subtle camera roll only during the Atlantic handoff. No generic wipes, bouncing cards, floating decorative labels, or scene-by-scene fades.

## Music and sound

Build a fresh score specifically around the story:
- 138–142 BPM;
- punchy breakbeat drums with a confident bassline;
- polished chopped rhythm texture, not comic plucks;
- quick tension dip for ringing;
- pulse remains alive under the call at reduced volume;
- the full groove returns when the email is sent;
- strongest lift at the 50+ languages reveal;
- short, premium final resolve.

Sound design is functional: one message send, subtle research ticks, one call-connect sound, paper/email motion, and four clean confirmation hits. No random “tun tun” interruptions and no novelty sounds.

Mix speech clearly at approximately -14 LUFS overall with true peak at or below -1 dBTP.

## Build approach
1. Rebuild the composition from scratch rather than modifying the current scene sequence.
2. Base the task screens on the actual Asmi canvas patterns: option rows, selection state, call stepper, activity events, email artifact, and completion message.
3. Use the existing short French call clips only after checking sentence timing and voice contrast; recut if any beat overstays its story purpose.
4. Create a small consistent image set only if the first frame tests show it improves realism.
5. Choreograph widescreen and vertical layouts independently around the same master object and story timing.
6. Compose a new score to the final edit rather than fitting animation to the previous music.
7. Render key-frame contact sheets at every transition, then revise collisions, dead space, pacing, and continuity before final export.
8. Verify both masters for duration, dimensions, H.264/AAC playback, 48 kHz audio, loudness, true peak, and exact call/music boundaries.

## Creative-director verification gates
- The viewer understands the gift story within the first three seconds.
- The film works as a story with the feature labels removed.
- Every product state matches a real Asmi behavior.
- Options are a choice inside Asmi, never a fake commerce checkout.
- Only two user actions occur: send the request and select the merchant.
- No visible live transcript appears.
- The phone call proof lasts about five seconds total.
- The same task visibly persists from iMessage request to completion.
- Every transition is causally connected; no scene feels like a new slide.
- The four colleagues introduced at the start are resolved at the payoff.
- “50+ languages” appears only after French has visibly completed the task.
- Both versions are readable without sound and feel energetic with sound.
- No empty banner frames, parked phone mockups, oversized text walls, or generic SaaS animation remain.
