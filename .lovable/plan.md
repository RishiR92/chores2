# Asmi launch film V7 - motion-picture reset

## Direction
Rebuild the film as a fast, playful **jazz-house chase**: live drums, walking bass, brass punches, handclaps, and a driving club pulse. The picture behaves like one continuous camera move through Asmi's pursuit, not a sequence of presentation slides.

Keep the dark base for contrast, but bring in warm flash-white, electric blue, call green, signal red, and brief golden brass accents. Use the real Asmi logo only at the finish. Typography stays compact and subordinate to the action.

## Why V6 feels flat
- The channel explanation is a centered headline above four evenly spaced icons, which reads like a process diagram.
- The task range, phone, proof, language, and achievements each reset to another centered dark composition.
- Most scenes enter, hold, and leave independently instead of physically causing the next scene.
- The phone outline is technically tall, but its small scale, flat rim, and front-on presentation keep it from feeling like a real iPhone in motion.
- The soundtrack supplies pulse, but not enough syncopation, lift, brass personality, or scene-specific payoff.

The final period becomes a red pursuit puck that never disappears. It punches through the frame and pulls the camera into the story.

### 2. Show the behavior through action - 0:02-0:05
Remove the explainer slide entirely.

The red puck races through four full-frame, realistic interface moments in one uninterrupted move:
1. It strikes the green Phone icon and the screen rings Mission Gym.
2. Voicemail ejects the puck sideways into a real Messages thread.
3. The blue send arrow fires it into a Mail compose sheet.
4. The mail arrow arcs back into a second call, which turns green on connection.

Words appear only as kinetic labels attached to the moving object: `call`, `text`, `email`, `call again`. Failed attempts remain briefly stamped behind the camera path. The camera finally pulls wide enough to reveal the complete message as one lockup:

`she tries every way in until it's done.`

This makes channel escalation understandable through cause and effect rather than a diagram.

### 5. Gym chase - 0:10-0:18

## Motion system
- **Default movement:** camera-led push, orbit, and rack-focus; objects travel in depth rather than sliding flat across the frame.
- **Signature transition:** the red pursuit puck physically becomes the next interface control or carries the current artifact into the next scene.
- **Readability rule:** camera and phone settle square before every important message, email, result, and number.
- **Rhythm:** alternate 4-8 frame punches with 18-30 frame readable landings; avoid repeated centered reveals, generic wipes, black resets, and isolated title cards.
- **Format adaptation:** choreograph vertical and widescreen separately around the same action path, rather than shrinking one layout into the other.

## Sound design
Compose a fresh, continuous jazz-house launch score around the locked edit at roughly 130 BPM:
- upright/electric bass riff and brushed count-in for suspense
- live kick, snare, claps, and syncopated hi-hats entering with channel escalation
- clipped brass stabs and piano jabs for the task avalanche
- locked groove under readable phone actions
- one-bar breath for the cancellation result
- polyrhythmic accents for languages
- biggest horn and bass impacts for `20,000` and `30M`
- sustained final chord over the real logo

No random beat drops, isolated melodic cues, call recordings, fake transcripts, or waveform visuals. Interface sounds remain restrained and land exactly with visible actions.
