# Asmi launch film V9 — high-energy consumer launch rebuild

## Creative verdict

V8 solved cleanliness, but it removed the thing people love about Asmi.

- **The world is too dark and serious.** One smoky studio plate remains behind nearly the entire film, so every beat feels emotionally identical.
- **The pace is simulated, not felt.** Most movement is a slow camera scale, fades, or screen wipes. The phone stays in nearly the same place while interfaces replace one another.
- **The opening is tasteful but not magnetic.** Small type on a dark background reads like a luxury teaser, not a consumer product people want to share.
- **Task breadth became paperwork.** Small white slips feel administrative rather than fun, fast, or rewarding.
- **The channel chase lacks escalation.** Phone, text, and email are readable, but each occupies a similar shot with similar timing; resistance never visibly raises the energy.
- **The scale claims are still stat slides.** A wall of tiny rectangles behind `20,000` and `30M` does not make either achievement feel culturally large.
- **The score is the biggest failure.** It is a sparse synthetic bed with isolated low hits, not a composed launch track. Its energy falls between beats instead of carrying the edit forward.

The website works because it combines bold Bricolage-style type, warm paper, blue/coral/citrus hits, cutout objects, tactile imperfection, cheeky copy, fast horizontal motion, and hard contrast between playful and dark sections. V9 will translate that personality into film craft rather than copying page layouts.

## New direction: Asmi takes over the frame

Build a **bright, kinetic, tactile product film** where a single request triggers an unstoppable chain reaction. It should feel young, culturally current, funny, and expensive—not childish, not a SaaS presentation, and not a luxury-tech mood reel.

The signature device is a **physical relay**: each failed channel is knocked out of the way by the next one. A call screen slams into voicemail; the voicemail label tears open into an iMessage thread; the blue send bubble races across frame and unfolds into Mail; the sent email wraps around the phone edge and reconnects as a green live call. The audience sees persistence rather than reading an explanation.

## Visual world

- Replace the smoky studio with fast-changing, full-frame **editorial sets**: warm paper, ink black, electric blue, coral, citrus, mint, and brief ultraviolet accents drawn from Asmi’s strongest website identity.
- Use real cutout textures, oversized printed type, torn-paper edges, ink registration, receipt stock, phone glass, and crisp shadows. Keep compositions spacious enough to feel designed, not scrapbooked.
- Keep the realistic full-height iPhone, but let it move: whip into frame, lock square for reading, rotate through match cuts, and become the anchor connecting physical and digital objects.
- Bring back Asmi’s visual wit through controlled scale surprises: a giant hold timer, a cancellation clause snapping into place, a refund receipt hitting like a finish-line ribbon.
- Use Bricolage-style grotesque campaign type, Space Mono for proof/details, native system type inside the phone, and the real Asmi wordmark only at the end.
- No dark smoky ambience, floating paperwork, generic card grids, repeated centered compositions, cartoon mascots, emoji decoration, confetti, or template transitions.

## 32-second story and pace

### 1. Cold open — 0:00–0:02

Three percussive visual cuts fill the frame:

`the most irritating`

`assistant.`

`for them.`

The word `them` is physically shoved aside by the incoming iMessage. Start energetic immediately—no slow atmospheric prelude.

### 2. Task rush — 0:02–0:05

Two conveyor-like streams of oversized task phrases and tactile cutouts race in opposite directions: flight, dentist, insurance, dinner, plumber, passport, vet, haircut, charge dispute. Icons remain embedded in the physical objects. The gym card gets caught by the moving phone and becomes the chosen story.

This should feel like the website’s marquee and cutout energy translated into a moving title sequence—not a task cloud or menu.

### 3. One-message handoff — 0:05–0:07.5

A realistic iMessage exchange lands in three sharp beats:

- `they won't let me cancel my gym. i've tried twice.`
- `send me your member ID.` / `GX-88214`
- `got it. don't think about it again.`

The final bubble accelerates into the channel chase.

### 4. Channel chase — 0:07.5–0:17.5

One escalating run with a distinct action for every channel:

1. **Call:** Mission Gym rings; a huge `VOICEMAIL` stamp physically interrupts it.
2. **Text:** the red call control tears into the blue Messages send control. The gym says cancellations must be in person; Asmi counters with written notice.
3. **Email:** the reply stretches into a real Mail compose sheet with `Cancellation notice - GX-88214`, section 4.2, and the $59 refund request.
4. **Call again:** the send arrow exits the phone, circles its edge as a fast blue light trace, and reconnects green.

The channel names may flash for only 4–6 frames as typographic impact labels, never as cards or a diagram. A continuous top-speed camera move and matched sound effects make the escalation unmistakable.

### 5. Win — 0:17.5–0:20

Everything stops for half a beat. Then three outcomes hit in rapid succession:

`cancelled.`

`$59 refunded.`

`GX-88214 confirmed.`

A coral rubber stamp lands: `she doesn't stop until it's done.` The result should feel satisfying and funny, not corporate.

### 6. More Asmi — 0:20–0:23

The cancellation receipt pulls a fast horizontal ribbon of three more wins through frame: flight rebooked, dentist confirmed, insurance claim approved. This proves the behavior repeats without returning to a feature list.

### 7. Languages — 0:23–0:25.5

One message wraps around the moving phone in Spanish, Hindi, Mandarin, Arabic, French, and Japanese. Each script inherits the previous word’s motion and resolves into:

`50+ languages.`

`different language. same chase.`

### 8. Cultural proof — 0:25.5–0:29.5

Rebuild the statistics as two physical spectacles:

- Thousands of completed-task receipts fire through a high-speed sorting mechanism; the camera pulls overhead and the sorted color blocks form `20,000`.
- The `20,000` grid flips like a split-flap display into a mosaic of real-looking social post crops, reactions, and view counters. A snap zoom reveals `30M` spanning the full frame.

Each gets one full musical downbeat and the line `in the past 30 days`. No generic number counter and no background wall of identical tiles.

### 9. End game — 0:29.5–0:32

The 30M mosaic collapses inward at speed and resolves into the real Asmi logo on clean warm paper—not another dark frame. The line lands with a final satisfying stamp/click:

`she doesn't stop until it's done.`

Then native iMessage and WhatsApp entry marks appear with `text asmi.`

## Music and sound — complete replacement

Do not synthesize another soundtrack from basic tones. Generate a studio-quality original track through ElevenLabs Music after linking ElevenLabs to the project.

### Music brief

- **128–132 BPM**, modern maximalist electro-pop / breakbeat launch music
- punchy acoustic-electronic drums, elastic bass, bright chopped synth hook, handclaps, tactile percussion, short brass-like synth stabs, and a euphoric final lift
- playful confidence and forward momentum; polished enough for a major consumer launch
- no vocals, no jazz, no cinematic drone, no trap clichés, no cartoon sounds, no random EDM drop, no “tun tun tun” melodic interruption
- structure composed to exact edit timings, with continuous propulsion rather than isolated impacts

### Energy map

- **0:00:** immediate two-beat hook; no ambient wait
- **0:02:** full groove enters with the task rush
- **0:07.5–0:17.5:** four rising eight-bar-style gear shifts synchronized to call → text → email → call again
- **0:17.5:** one deliberate quarter-beat vacuum before the win
- **0:18–0:25.5:** hook returns brighter with added percussion
- **0:25.5 and 0:27.8:** the two largest musical impacts for `20,000` and `30M`
- **0:29.5:** final harmonic lift, then a dry logo click at 0:31.8

Add tightly synchronized UI and tactile sound design: message sends, phone vibration, voicemail interruption, paper rip, email whoosh, call connection, stamp, receipt sorter, split-flap motion, and logo click. Music remains dominant; effects sharpen transitions without turning into a sound-effects demo.

Master at 48 kHz stereo, approximately -14 LUFS integrated, true peak at or below -1 dBTP.

## Technical execution

- Rebuild the Remotion composition as V9 while preserving V8 and every earlier master.
- Create separate vertical and widescreen choreography rather than scaling one layout.
- Use the existing cutout assets and website palette as source material, but create film-specific compositions and motion.
- Keep native Phone, Messages, and Mail surfaces readable whenever they carry story-critical text.
- Generate the final score only after the silent animatic timing is locked, then cut picture and sound together at frame-level sync points.

## Review gates

1. Render a 6-second opening/task-rush motion test; reject it if it still feels like slides or a website recording.
2. Render the full silent animatic; the call → text → email → call-again escalation must be obvious without sound.
3. Review the realistic phone and every critical message at full speed in both formats.
4. Generate and audition the new music against the locked animatic; revise the track if it does not create immediate forward momentum.
5. Inspect full-speed renders and contact sheets for dead frames, repetitive framing, unreadable UI, or cartoon/template motion.
6. Verify 32 seconds, 30 fps, 1080×1920 and 1920×1080, H.264, AAC stereo, 48 kHz, loudness, and safe peaks before delivery.
