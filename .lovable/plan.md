# Asmi social film — when AI calls AI

## Core idea

Create a new **30-second, vertical-first social film** around a real-world observation:

> Asmi has been handling the world for thousands of people. Lately, more of the voices answering her calls are AI too.

The film should make this feel fascinating and useful—not dystopian, technical, or gimmicky. The point is not “look, two bots.” The point is that Asmi can navigate whoever answers and still move a real task forward.

Preserve all earlier films. This becomes a separate, versioned composition and export.

## Story and exact copy

### 1. Proof first — 0:00–0:04

Open at full pace on a stream of completed real-world outcomes: `BOOKED`, `QUOTE HELD`, `DETAILS FOUND`, `CALL BACK READY`.

Copy lands in three tactile beats:

- `asmi has been handling the real world`
- `for thousands of people.`
- `then something changed.`

The final line is interrupted by an incoming call—not a title-card transition.

### 2. The reveal — 0:04–0:11

A realistic Asmi call screen moves into frame. A second business phone surface answers from the opposite side.

Use the strongest clean exchange from the Herman’s recording:

- Asmi: `“I’m an AI receptionist. An AI calling on behalf of John.”`
- Herman’s: `“Yes, of course. I can help you with a booking… What can I do for John today?”`

On-screen copy, timed to the exchange:

- `AI CALLING.`
- `AI ANSWERING.`
- `yeah. that's happening now.`

The two labels should feel like physical call-identification stamps, not chat bubbles or a diagram.

### 3. This is already useful — 0:11–0:18

Stay inside the same call rather than resetting the scene. Show the useful details being resolved through tactile food/menu objects and a concise booking brief:

- `fully vegan`
- `rotating buffet`
- `august 21`
- `guest count needed`

Use one short, complete exchange from the call where Herman’s explains the menu and Asmi closes with a clear next step. Keep only the most impressive sentence pair; never cut a speaker mid-sentence.

Resolve to:

- `not a demo.`
- `a real question. a useful answer.`

### 4. It is happening across the real world — 0:18–0:25

The restaurant brief folds into the second real call without a generic wipe. A menu strip physically becomes a measured garden/property strip.

Use the cleanest outcome-producing exchange from the gardening recording:

- Asmi: `“Terry's looking for a quote to trim down a 5,000-square-yard property.”`
- Service: `“Let's get someone out there for an estimate this Thursday, around 2 in the afternoon.”`
- Asmi: `“Thursday at 2 p.m. works really well.”`

Show the real result forming while the voices speak:

- `5,000 SQ YD`
- `THURSDAY · 2 PM`
- `ESTIMATE SLOT HELD`

### 5. Insight and brand resolve — 0:25–0:30

Both calls collapse into one clean pursuit line and then the real Asmi wordmark.

Final copy:

- `the other side of the call is changing.`
- `the outcome isn't.`
- `asmi.`
- `built for the real world. whoever answers.`

Close with the existing accurate Messages and WhatsApp marks and `text asmi.`

## Visual direction

Use the approved V9/V11 language: warm paper, ink, electric blue, coral, citrus, mint; Bricolage Grotesque for campaign type; Space Mono for proof; native system type inside realistic phone surfaces; tactile print texture, crisp shadows, slight type rotation, hard editorial cuts, and the existing snappy spring behavior.

Evolve it specifically for this story:

- **One physical call line:** a bold cable/signal path connects Asmi’s phone to the business phone and carries every transition.
- **Two credible endpoints:** realistic call surfaces with restrained labels; no robot heads, glowing brains, sci-fi grids, chatbots, or fake dashboards.
- **Dialogue becomes matter:** selected spoken phrases turn into physical menu strips, booking slips, address fields, and appointment stamps.
- **No live transcript treatment:** only short, exact, sentence-level captions synchronized to the real audio so the story remains clear sound-off.
- **No slide structure:** every result from one exchange must physically cause the next shot.
- **Social-safe framing:** essential copy stays clear of the top 250 px and bottom 320 px in the vertical master; phones and captions remain readable at feed size.

## Sound and edit

- Keep the approved energetic electro-pop/breakbeat personality, recut around the real calls rather than letting music overpower them.
- Start with immediate percussion and forward motion.
- Duck music decisively beneath each authentic exchange; restore energy on each resolved detail or booking stamp.
- Select clips only at natural sentence boundaries using the recordings’ pauses. No chopped final words or abrupt breaths.
- Clean and level both mono recordings, reduce noise conservatively, and preserve their real-call character.
- Use subtle ring, connect, paper, measurement, booking-stamp, and dry logo-click effects; audio dialogue remains the hero.
- Master at 48 kHz stereo, approximately -14 LUFS integrated, with true peak at or below -1 dBTP.

## Technical execution

- Add both supplied recordings as source assets for the new Remotion film.
- Audition and timestamp every candidate excerpt before editing; the transcript guides selection, but the audible sentence ending controls the cut.
- Build a new composition rather than altering `ChaseEngineFilm`, V9, V11, or their delivered masters.
- Create separate vertical 1080×1920 and widescreen 1920×1080 choreography at 30 fps; do not crop one into the other.
- Keep the finished duration between 28 and 32 seconds, H.264 video with AAC 48 kHz stereo audio.

## Verification gates

1. **Claim check:** use “thousands of people” as the user-supplied claim; do not invent call counts, growth rates, dates, or percentages.
2. **Audio truth:** every subtitle matches the recording exactly; both excerpts end on complete sentences; no synthetic transcript is presented as spoken audio.
3. **Five-second test:** a sound-off viewer understands that Asmi handles real-world calls for people and is now encountering AI on the other side.
4. **Value test:** after one watch, viewers can name two concrete outcomes—useful restaurant information and a Thursday 2 PM estimate slot.
5. **Design test:** reject any frame resembling a deck, dashboard, waveform visualizer, generic AI ad, or sci-fi explainer.
6. **Phone-size test:** review the vertical master at actual social-feed size; all critical phone content and captions must be readable.
7. **Audio test:** audition on phone speakers and headphones; voices stay intelligible, music ducks cleanly, and no sentence is cut off.
8. **Delivery test:** inspect key-frame contact sheets and full-speed exports; verify duration, resolution, 30 fps, H.264/AAC, 48 kHz, loudness, and peak safety for both formats.
