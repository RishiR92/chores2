# Asmi 50+ Languages Feature Film — V3 Rebuild

## Goal
Create a premium, fast, consumer-grade feature film that proves one clear capability through Asmi’s real product flow:

> A user in New York asks Asmi to send great wine to four colleagues in Paris. Asmi researches local options, lets the user choose, calls the shop in French, follows up by email in French, and returns the completed order details.

The film should feel like a real feature demonstration—not a brand banner, a fake phone commercial, or an abstract language montage.

## What changes from V2

### Remove
- The invented checklist inside iMessage: “searching independent paris wine shops / local delivery available / calling now.”
- The long, staged phone conversation.
- Four invented recipient-confirmation cards.
- Large static headline-plus-phone compositions that read like banner ads.
- Burgundy/navy gradient backgrounds and decorative texture that compete with the flow.
- The current late-track sound, comic pops, and repeated notification effects.
- Any visible live transcript inside Asmi. The real product explicitly summarizes outcomes rather than exposing live transcripts.

### Preserve
- The NYC-to-Paris use case.
- Caves Bernard as the illustrative independent Paris merchant.
- French spoken audio with concise English editorial subtitles.
- French written confirmation.
- The closing capability: Asmi talks, texts, and emails in 50+ languages.
- Separate widescreen and vertical compositions.

## Real Asmi workflow to show

The flow will match the existing product behavior:

```text
User texts Asmi
      ↓
The message opens Asmi’s real task canvas with a task-specific choice view
      ↓
User taps the preferred local merchant
      ↓
Asmi starts the action and shows live status—not a transcript
      ↓
Call outcome becomes a written follow-up
      ↓
Asmi returns one compact confirmation receipt in chat
```

This is grounded in the current Asmi product:
- Research tasks surface tappable options rather than paragraphs.
- The user chooses or lets Asmi choose.
- Calls progress through queued, dialing, ringing, connected, and wrapping states.
- The live transcript is not shown.
- Email/message activity appears as channel events or artifacts.
- Completion returns a concise receipt or confirmation to the user.

## Story and copy — approximately 27 seconds

### 1. Cold open: the ask — 0:00–0:03
A real iMessage conversation fills the frame immediately. No separate title card.

**User:**
“send great wine to 4 colleagues in paris tomorrow. local place. no generic gift box.”

Purpose: establish distance, urgency, and the task in under three seconds.

### 2. Asmi builds the choice — 0:03–0:07

- calls welcomed for advice
- delivery available on request

Caves Bernard moves into focus as Asmi’s recommended option.

**Asmi:**
“found three local cavistes. this one actually wants a phone call.”

One visible tap selects **Caves Bernard**. This is the only user action after the initial message.

### 3. Action starts — 0:07–0:09
The selected merchant card compresses into the live task surface.

Visible state progression:
**queued → dialing → ringing → connected**

Copy outside the phone stays minimal:
**“asmi takes it from here.”**

The phone remains credible, but the camera moves through it rather than parking it beside a headline.

### 4. French call highlight — 0:09–0:14
Keep the whole proof to roughly five seconds:
1. Merchant pickup: “Caves Bernard, bonjour.” — about 0.8 seconds.
2. Asmi’s essential request in French — about 2.3 seconds.
3. Merchant confirms four bottles and next-day delivery — about 1.8 seconds.

The merchant must sound like a real shop owner answering during business: older voice, slight room ambience, natural pickup breath, slower than Asmi, not studio-clean.

The Asmi canvas shows only call state, elapsed time, merchant identity, and a compact waveform. The real audio is used as cinematic proof; English subtitles sit in the film’s editorial layer—not inside Asmi as a fake transcript.

The two key subtitle beats:
- “Four excellent bottles for four colleagues, delivered tomorrow.”
- “Yes. I can arrange that.”

### 5. Call becomes action — 0:14–0:18
The final merchant waveform straightens into the underline of a French email. This is one continuous transformation, not a cut to a new slide.

The Asmi canvas creates a compact email artifact showing only the essential details:
- recipient: Caves Bernard

- four recipient names/addresses represented compactly
- delivery window and reference

The send action fires; the email artifact folds back into Asmi’s timeline as a completed event. No separate operating-system Mail app is shown.

**“call done. details confirmed in french.”**

### 6. Real completion receipt — 0:18–0:21
Return to the same Asmi task canvas—not a separate grid.

One receipt appears:
- **Caves Bernard**
- four bottles
- four Paris recipients

**Asmi:**
“sorted. four bottles. four colleagues. zero midnight french for you.”

This is the trustworthy ending of the example.

### 7. Feature reveal — 0:21–0:25
The French phrases from the call—not random decorative words—split into equivalent channel actions across several major languages.

The same compact task receipt rapidly switches language while its structure stays fixed. Phone, text, and email icons orbit through the receipt, proving that the capability spans channels rather than merely translating text.

Build the statement in one fluid move:

**talks. texts. emails.**

Then the language count lands with the strongest musical hit:

**50+ languages.**

Supporting line:
**“ask in yours. asmi handles theirs.”**

### 8. End — 0:25–0:27
The completed receipt collapses into the real Asmi logo.

Only two lines:
- **asmi**
- **your agent for the real world.**

Native iMessage and WhatsApp marks appear with **“text asmi.”**

No extra feature list or repeated explanation.

## Visual direction

### Concept: International Dispatch
A kinetic, tactile cross-border operation with polished consumer-product clarity. It should feel like sending one message sets a highly competent system in motion across the Atlantic.

### Palette
Replace the dark wine-banner palette with a brighter, sharper system:
- **Pearl white `#F7F7F2`** — primary canvas and breathing room
- **Near-black `#111214`** — typography and device depth
- **Signal cobalt `#1254FF`** — Asmi/user action and route energy
- **Vermilion `#FF4F3D`** — urgency and decisive transitions
- **Champagne `#E8CB78`** — Paris/merchant accent, used sparingly
- **Success green `#35C98A`** — completion only

No purple haze, navy-to-burgundy banners, beige-dominated frames, glowing blobs, or decorative dot fields.

### Typography
- **Primary display:** Bricolage Grotesque ExtraBold for short, punchy feature statements.
- **Product/UI:** SF Pro–style system type for authentic Messages, calls, status, and Mail surfaces.
- **Utility labels:** Noto Sans Mono for times, locations, references, and channel states.
- No full-screen paragraphs. No words appearing as isolated slideshow lines.
- Copy enters as part of actions: route labels, selected cards, call states, email fields, and receipts.

### Composition
- Treat the phone as a moving camera portal, not a product mockup parked on a background.
- Widescreen: lateral NYC-to-Paris movement; task choices and action status use the extra horizontal space.
- Vertical: phone and generated view travel through depth; no scaled crop from widescreen.
- Keep primary action center-safe for LinkedIn/X and readable without sound.

## Motion system

### Default movement
- Fast physical handoffs with controlled inertia.
- Elements enter through directional travel tied to the story: NYC left, Paris right.
- Settling uses restrained springs; no bouncy cartoon motion.

### Signature transitions
1. **Message → choice view:** the blue message bubble unfolds horizontally into merchant cards.
2. **Choice → action:** the selected card snaps into the phone’s active-task header while rejected cards fall into depth.
3. **Ringing → connected:** the route line locks into a pulse ring around the merchant avatar.
4. **Call → email:** the final waveform becomes the email subject underline, then the Mail surface builds around it.
5. **Receipt → language reveal:** the fixed receipt structure remains while language and channel layers rotate through it.
6. **Feature → logo:** the three channel marks converge into the Asmi wordmark baseline.

### Scene-specific energy
- Ask: immediate, effortless.
- Choices: fast, intelligent, satisfying.
- Call: compressed suspense and credibility.
- Email: precise and frictionless.
- Result: clean payoff.
- Languages: fastest, most euphoric visual section.
- End: short, confident resolve.

### Effects
- Subtle device reflections, dimensional shadows, print-like micrograin, and shallow depth shifts.
- One transatlantic route stroke used as a narrative object across scenes.
- No generic particles, floating dots, random rings, decorative gradients, or unrelated flashes.
- Motion blur simulated through directional duplicate trails only on the fastest two transitions.

## Music and sound design

### New score
Create a completely new 136–140 BPM instrumental launch track:
- tight breakbeat/acoustic-electronic drums
- elastic bassline
- clipped guitar or dry synth rhythm
- restrained French-touch filter movement
- bright percussive detail
- no vocals
- no accordion, lounge-jazz pastiche, comic marimba/plucks, trap hats, or generic EDM drop

### Musical arc
- 0:00: message lands on the first transient.
- 0:03: groove opens as options appear.
- 0:07: bass cuts briefly for dialing tension.
- 0:09–0:14: score ducks but keeps a pulse under the short call.
- 0:14: beat returns when the waveform becomes the email.
- 0:18: clean completion hit—not a cartoon chime.
- 0:21: arrangement expands for the multilingual feature reveal.
- 0:25: final branded resolve with a tight two-second ending.

### Mix
- Speech always intelligible without killing momentum.
- Duck music to roughly 35–40% during the five-second call, not near-silence.
- Use one restrained call-connect sound and one completion sound.
- Master both cuts at approximately **-14 LUFS**, **≤ -1 dBTP**, 48 kHz stereo.

## Production approach

1. Recut/regenerate the French dialogue into a five-second highlight with clearly different Asmi and merchant performances.
2. Replace the V2 banner visual system with the International Dispatch palette and motion language.
3. Build one continuous stateful hero surface that progresses through real Asmi states instead of isolated scene cards.
4. Recreate the generated merchant-choice view using the app’s existing option-selection patterns.
5. Show only real live-action status during the call; keep subtitles editorial and never present them as an Asmi transcript.
6. Return the result as one authentic confirmation artifact inside the same conversation.
7. Build the multilingual reveal from the actual call/email content and channel actions.
8. Compose widescreen and vertical independently.
9. Render key-frame reviews before final masters, checking phone proportions, text collisions, subtitle safety, transition continuity, and silent comprehension.
10. Render and verify both masters for codec, duration, dimensions, loudness, true peak, and audio/scene boundaries.

## Quality gates

- A viewer understands the full workflow with sound off.
- The flow matches the existing Asmi product behavior.
- The user acts only twice: sends the request and taps the preferred option.
- No live transcript appears.
- The total real call proof is about five seconds.
- Every transition is caused by the previous action.
- No section reads like a banner or presentation slide.
- Merchant and Asmi voices are immediately distinguishable.
- Music stays energetic through the call and ends confidently.
- “50+ languages” is earned by the story before it is stated.
- Both formats are inspected frame-by-frame at all scene boundaries before delivery.
