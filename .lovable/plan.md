# asmi 45-second launch film

Create two finished edits from one visual and audio system:
- **Vertical:** 1080 × 1920 for Reels, TikTok, Shorts, and mobile sharing
- **Widescreen:** 1920 × 1080 for the website, decks, demos, and launch screens
- **Length:** approximately 45 seconds at 30fps
- **Audio:** hype instrumental, real call snippets, message pops, and restrained interface sound design; no narrator

## Creative direction: “polite chaos, handled”

The film should feel like asmi’s site came alive: bold paper-and-ink graphics, electric accent colors, oversized lowercase copy, chat bubbles, real-world cutouts, rapid task updates, and dry humor. It should feel youthful and fast without becoming noisy or looking like a generic AI commercial.

**Palette**
- Paper: `#FBF7F0`
- Ink: `#141318`
- Electric blue: `#2F5BFF`
- Coral: `#FF5A47`
- Citrus: `#E8FF5A`
- Mint success: `#35D6A4`

**Typography**
- Display: Bricolage Grotesque / Archivo Black energy for blunt, oversized statements
- Wordmark and occasional editorial contrast: Instrument Serif italic
- Utility/status copy: Space Mono

**Visual motifs**
1. Legal-pad grid, paper grain, torn edges, stamps, and marker swipes from the site
2. Message bubbles that become task canvases, call states, and completion receipts
3. Channel relay lines: call → voicemail → text → email → done

**Motion system**
- Default entrance: fast masked rise with a slight scale settle
- Accent entrance: overshooting spring for key words, message bubbles, and success stamps
- Exit: directional push or paper tear, never a fade-to-black
- Scene transitions: chat bubble expansion, hard paper wipes, and match-cuts between circles/icons
- Continuous motion: subtle texture drift, tiny parallax, live timers, waveform movement, and active status pulses
- Similar elements always animate consistently; no random transition collection

## Storyboard and exact pacing

### 0:00–0:03 — Pattern interrupt
**Visual:** The screen starts mid-chaos: tiny fragments of “on hold,” “voicemail,” “no slots,” and “please call us” stack for less than a second, then get shoved aside by the asmi wordmark.

**Copy:**
> the most irritating assistant in the world.

On the beat, a smaller line stamps underneath:
> annoying to them. invisible to you.

**Sound:** Immediate music hook, short telephone click, one message pop.

### 0:03–0:09 — The ask becomes a visual plan
**Visual:** An iMessage-style bubble enters:
> bars near me with actual veg food. table for 5.

The bubble stretches into the site’s visual restaurant picker. Three image-led options arrive right-to-left; ratings, distance, hours, and “her pick” appear only after the cards settle. A fingerless selection pulse lands on Wayfare Tavern.

**Copy:**
> no ten tabs. no wall of text.

Then:
> she plans it with you.

**Sound:** Music opens up; three soft card ticks and one confident selection thump.

### 0:09–0:13 — Intent turns into action
**Visual:** “Wayfare Tavern” compresses into a call chip. The restaurant card flips into a live call state with a timer, animated ring, and waveform. No fake live transcript is shown.

**Copy:**
> then she gets it done.

Status beats land quickly:
- calling now
- on hold
- table for 5, 7:30

**Sound:** Music ducks sharply; use a short, intelligible real call moment where the booking/result is clearest.

### 0:13–0:21 — The chase engine
**Visual:** A single task canvas fills the frame: “book a dentist. mornings only.” Four channel steps animate sequentially, with failed states crossing out and the successful step snapping into mint.

**On-screen updates:**
- clinic 1 - no morning slot
- clinic 2 - went to voicemail
- emailed clinic 3
- clinic 4 - 8:30am tues confirmed

Final receipt:
> booked. already in your calendar.

**Copy transition:**
> call. voicemail. email. call again.

**Sound:** A second real call excerpt sits above heavily ducked music. Voicemail and email steps use tiny, tasteful cues rather than novelty effects.

### 0:21–0:28 — Range, without a feature list
**Visual:** Three fast task bubbles arrive as if typed by different people. Each expands into one clear physical-world action, then resolves into a receipt:
1. “AC is dead. need a tech ASAP.” → live HVAC call → “saturday 9am. booked.”
2. “check on grandpa in Sevilla - in Spanish.” → Spanish call ring → “medicine taken. feeling okay.”
3. “why was i charged $60?” → bank + merchant threads → “$60 back.”

**Copy:**
> whatever it takes is kind of the point.

**Sound:** Use the strongest short excerpts from the existing HVAC and Spanish call recordings. The music ducks to near-minimum under every voice and returns between snippets.

### 0:28–0:34 — Parallel work
**Visual:** One request branches into three simultaneous call lanes for plumbers. The lanes show clear live states rather than dense copy:
- plumber A - $180, 6pm
- plumber B - booked out
- plumber C - still on hold

The winning lane pulls forward and becomes:
> plumber at your door, 6pm today.

**Copy:**
> she runs the threads. you get the answer.

**Sound:** Percussion accelerates; three restrained ring pulses pan slightly across the mix, then collapse into one completion hit.

### 0:34–0:39 — Breadth and languages
**Visual:** A dense, deliberate cluster inspired by the website—not a scattered cloud—builds around the center. English, Mandarin, Hindi, Spanish, Arabic, French, Bengali, Portuguese, and other major languages pack tightly into an ellipse. Individual languages bubble forward in coral one at a time; the cluster then compresses into “50+ languages.”

Behind or immediately after it, short task labels rotate quickly:
- cancel my gym
- chase my insurance claim
- renew my passport
- find a mover
- get a vet slot

**Copy:**
> your chores. their language. handled.

**Sound:** Music reaches its highest energy; each language pop follows a rhythmic subdivision rather than random timing.

### 0:39–0:45 — Brand payoff
**Visual:** The task labels fly inward and form a clean asmi wordmark. Coral marker stroke draws underneath. iMessage and WhatsApp icons settle below as the only closing actions.

**Closing copy:**
> she doesn't stop until it's done.

Then the final lockup:
> text asmi.

**Sound:** Final beat resolves cleanly; message pop lands on the icon reveal; music ends with a short branded tail rather than fading away vaguely.

## Audio treatment

- Use the existing original doctor, Marco HVAC, and Spanish grandpa recordings; preserve the correct source moments already identified in the project.
- Normalize dialogue by speaker, not merely by file, so the user and asmi voices land at the same perceived loudness.
- Target clear, consistent speech around broadcast-safe dialogue levels, control peaks, and verify every excerpt on both headphones and a phone speaker.
- Duck the music aggressively during calls, with smooth ramps before and after each excerpt; voices must remain unquestionably dominant.
- Keep real call excerpts short and purposeful—only enough to prove that asmi is actually acting in the real world.
- Use the existing message sounds sparingly. Add only a few tactile cues for selection, status, and completion.
- Start from the current track only if it passes the energy test; otherwise create or source a punchier instrumental with an immediate hook, rhythmic midsection, no vocals, and a clean 45-second ending.

## Format adaptation

The widescreen and vertical edits will share timing, copy, and audio, but not simply crop one another.

**Vertical**
- Full-frame phone/chat moments
- One dominant idea per screen
- Large text inside mobile-safe margins
- Stacked task lanes and compact receipts

**Widescreen**
- Asymmetric editorial layouts
- Product view on one side, kinetic headline on the other
- Parallel calls can spread horizontally
- More negative space and larger real-world cutout motion

All text will remain readable without audio, and all important action will stay inside social-platform safe areas.

## Quality checks before delivery

- Render and inspect stills at the opening, each task transition, every call, language cluster, and final lockup in both formats.
- Watch the full film muted to confirm the story remains obvious.
- Listen on headphones and simulated phone-speaker output to confirm the real calls remain clear and equally loud.
- Check that the background track never overpowers dialogue.
- Verify names, times, outcomes, logo scale, line breaks, and text-safe margins.
- Confirm no frame resembles a static slide, no text clips, and no mobile crop loses important information.
- Deliver two final MP4s in `/mnt/documents/`, clearly named for vertical and widescreen use.
