# Asmi multilingual Paris wine delivery film

## Creative concept

Build a fresh **New York-to-Paris caper** rather than reusing the gym or AI-to-AI visual worlds. One message leaves a rainy-blue NYC evening, crosses into a warm Paris wine-shop world, and returns as a completed delivery. The film’s signature transition is **language becoming action**: English words travel across the Atlantic, reform into spoken French, then become a French confirmation email.

The story will use **Caves Bernard**, an independent Paris caviste whose public site explicitly invites customers to call for advice and offers local delivery on request. The exact order, bottle recommendations, colleague names, price, and confirmation will be clearly fictionalized for the film; the merchant will not be contacted or represented as endorsing Asmi.

## Story and copy — approximately 27–30 seconds

### 1. The impossible-from-here ask — 0:00–0:04

Open in New York at 6:42 p.m. A realistic iMessage arrives:

> need great wine delivered to 4 colleagues in paris tomorrow. somewhere local — not a generic gift site.

Paris is already after midnight. Four small colleague/address markers appear across a tactile street-map fragment, immediately establishing distance, time pressure, and four recipients.

### 2. The search narrows — 0:04–0:08

Asmi searches visually through noisy generic results, removes shops with ordinary online checkout, and lands on one human route:

> found an independent paris caviste. local delivery is arranged on request.

The selected shop card becomes a real phone surface instead of stopping as a search-results screen.

### 3. Asmi calls in French — 0:08–0:14

Show a credible live call to **Caves Bernard**. No fabricated “real recording”; use clean designed call audio and concise French dialogue with English subtitles:

> Bonjour — je cherche quatre bouteilles à livrer demain à Paris, pour quatre collègues. Quelque chose de très bon, mais pas trop formel.

The caviste confirms a curated mixed selection and delivery availability. The visual focus stays on the call, recipient count, timing, and recommendation—not a transcript wall.

### 4. The handoff becomes a French confirmation — 0:14–0:20

The spoken French waveform folds into a real Mail compose surface. Asmi sends the merchant a concise French confirmation containing:

- four recipients
- delivery addresses or one agreed Paris destination
- selected bottles
- delivery date/window
- gift note
- payment/confirmation reference

The email is readable, realistic, and visibly in French. A sent-mail streak travels through the Paris map and reaches all four recipients.

### 5. Done across an ocean — 0:20–0:24

Four delivery confirmations land in rhythm:

> 4 bottles selected.
>
> delivery confirmed.
>
> paris, tomorrow.

Then Asmi replies to the user in English:

> sorted. four bottles, four colleagues, zero midnight french for you.

### 6. Feature reveal — 0:24–0:28

The exact French phrases from the call and email expand into a fast global chorus of major scripts, then resolve cleanly to:

> talks. texts. emails.
>
> in 50+ languages.

Supporting line:

> ask in yours. asmi handles theirs.

### 7. End — 0:28–0:30

Real Asmi logo, one concise finish:

> your agent for the real world.

Use native iMessage and WhatsApp marks with `text asmi.`

## Visual direction

- Create a **transatlantic editorial thriller-comedy**: cool cobalt/steel NYC, warm burgundy/champagne Paris, crisp ivory communication surfaces, and one vivid chartreuse confirmation accent.
- Use postal labels, wine-shop paper, foil-stamped bottle silhouettes, map coordinates, phone glass, and moving type as cinematic materials—not decorative cards.
- Make the Atlantic crossing one continuous camera move. English message fragments stretch into a route line, become French phonetics around the phone, and resolve into written French in Mail.
- Keep the four bottles visually distinct through restrained neck-label shapes and recipient markers; avoid fake product brands or identifiable bottle labels.
- Use a credible tall iPhone and native-looking Messages, Phone, and Mail surfaces. Critical text gets a steady readable landing before motion resumes.
- Compose widescreen and vertical separately. Vertical emphasizes the phone and north-south NYC/Paris movement; widescreen uses the geographic distance and four-recipient spread.
- Avoid the older task conveyor, multicolor language bands, paper-card grids, giant stat treatment, and repeated channel-title rhythm. Preserve only the quality bar: clear story, physical transitions, realistic interfaces, bold readable copy, and a clean logo finish.

## Music and sound

- New original score tailored to this concept: **132–136 BPM French-touch percussion with modern breakbeat energy**, elegant bass, clipped guitar/synth rhythm, and bright tactile accents.
- Immediate intrigue in New York; forward acceleration during search; the groove narrows and ducks under the French call; a satisfying lift carries the email and four confirmations; the biggest musical release lands on `50+ languages`.
- No reused track, jazz pastiche, accordion cliché, vocals, random beat drops, cartoon effects, or dead air.
- Add restrained message, dialing, connection, email-send, map-routing, and delivery-confirmation sounds. Dialogue remains clean and intelligible.
- Master at 48 kHz stereo, around -14 LUFS integrated, with true peak at or below -1 dBTP.

## Production and verification

- Create a new Remotion composition while preserving all previous films and exports.
- Produce original French dialogue audio after checking grammar and natural spoken phrasing; subtitles must match exactly.
- Treat the transaction as a dramatized scenario based on publicly listed merchant capabilities, not as proof that the merchant completed this specific order.
- Render separate 1080×1920 vertical and 1920×1080 widescreen masters at 30 fps, H.264/AAC.
- Review the full film with sound and muted; verify French legibility, phone proportions, no overlaps, no clipped text, one continuous story, exact audio/scene boundaries, and strong feature comprehension at first viewing.
