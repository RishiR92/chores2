# Asmi — "she's not always talking to a human anymore" — social story concept

## Status
Concept only. No Remotion composition built yet — this locks story, copy, and verification gates before any build. Built on the approved V9/V11 visual language and the Chase Engine motion system; does not replace either film.

## Source review
- V9 (`HighlightVideoV11.tsx` + v9 plan): bright/tactile/kinetic identity — paper, ink, blue/coral/citrus/mint, Bricolage display type, Space Mono for evidence, real iPhone chrome, physical-relay channel transitions.
- Chase Engine plan (`asmi-chase-engine-social-launch-film-2026-09-18.md`): one continuous real-world pursuit across call → text → email → call, proven with a persistent chase line and elapsed-time ribbon, no diagrams or dashboards.
- Existing call assets (`hvac-call.mp4`, `doc-sandra-call.mp4`, `spanish-grandpa-call.mp4` and their trimmed excerpts): all are **Asmi-to-human** conversations (a provider dispatcher, a doctor's office staffer, a grandparent's caregiver contact). None currently on file is an Asmi-to-AI-agent call. This concept therefore assumes the two "authentic call snippets" it uses will be **newly supplied recordings of real calls where the other side was an automated/AI agent** (e.g., an AI-staffed booking line, an AI scheduling assistant, an AI-answered customer service queue). Until those are supplied and cleared, no dialogue is invented — placeholders are marked `[VERIFY]`.

## Story thesis
Asmi has handled thousands of real human calls for real people. Lately, more of those calls connect to something on the other end that isn't a person at all — another AI, doing someone else's real-world work the same way Asmi does yours. This film doesn't argue that AI-to-AI is the future; it shows, with two real recorded moments, that it is already quietly happening inside ordinary errands — and that Asmi handles it exactly like she handles everything else: fast, plainly, and until it's done. The insight is observational and current, not speculative: "the person you're trying to reach for you might already have their own Asmi."

## What must be verified before this ships
1. **Consent and rights** for both call recordings — confirm the business/counterparty consented to recording and to commercial use, per applicable two-party-consent state and platform rules.
2. **Confirm the counterparty was in fact an AI agent**, not a slow human or a scripted IVR tree — via the business's own disclosure, an explicit on-call statement ("you're speaking with an automated assistant"), or documented account from Asmi's call logs. Do not infer "AI" from voice quality alone.
3. **Verify the outcome was real and completed** (booking, confirmation, refund, etc.) with the same evidentiary bar the Chase Engine plan uses — no invented confirmation numbers or times.
4. **Confirm frequency claim before asserting "increasingly"** — pull actual internal data on the rate of AI-to-AI connections over a real recent window (e.g., "X% of calls this month connected to an automated system") before putting any number on screen. If data isn't ready, keep the claim qualitative ("more and more often") rather than numeric.
5. **Get final legal/brand sign-off on naming any real company** heard in the clips, or blur/rename per standard practice used for HVAC/doctor's office clips already in the library.
6. **Confirm which two clips are "impressive enough" to use** — the bar is: audio is intelligible, the AI-agent identity is unambiguous, and the moment shows a fast, clean resolution (not a stilted or confusing exchange). If neither existing new clip clears this bar, the film should ship with one clip and one dramatized-but-labeled reenactment, never two fabricated exchanges.

## Sound-off comprehension criteria (must pass on one silent watch)
A viewer with sound off, reading only on-screen text and phone UI, must be able to answer:
1. What has Asmi been doing for people historically? *(handling real calls for thousands of users)*
2. What's the new, specific observation? *(some of those calls now connect to another AI, not a person)*
3. Is this asserted as rare/one-off, or as a pattern? *(a pattern the film calls out plainly — "more often than you'd think" or the verified stat)*
4. Did both showcased moments end in a real, concrete outcome? *(yes — each clip closes on a native confirmation card, not an ambiguous cutaway)*
5. Does anything change about what Asmi does for the user because of this? *(no — she still finishes the job regardless of who/what answers)*
6. How do you get this? *(text Asmi via Messages/WhatsApp, shown at the very end)*

The film fails the gate if a silent viewer walks away thinking this is a warning about AI, a feature announcement, or a stat-first infographic — it must read as "here's something Asmi has quietly noticed while doing her job."
